The watch screen, running the real control law on a simulated heart-rate trace at 20× speed. Watch it hold steady through a spike.

It will never cut off a song. It cannot.

The part that chooses music is handed an interface with two things on it: read what is playing, and add something to the queue. Pause, skip and seek are not withheld by policy — they are absent. A skip from the automatic loop is a compile error, not a bug report.

So it waits. The decision lands in the last twenty seconds of whatever is playing, when it knows what you are actually doing, and the next track is already queued before the current one ends.

Five playlists. Yours.

You write the prompts. What Tempo means is whatever your Tempo prompt pulled out of your own library — not a genre, not someone else's idea of intensity. The system supplies the timing. You supply the taste.

It waits before it moves

A twenty-second climb does not change the music. Only a sustained one does — and never more than one zone at a time.

Take it back anytime

Turn the crown to pin a zone. It stays pinned until you say otherwise — no timer quietly handing control back.

Leave the phone

Heart rate, decisions and playback all run on the watch. The phone is needed once, to connect Spotify, and never again.

No account. No server. No copy of you.

There is no backend, because there is nothing for one to do. The watch talks to Spotify directly with your own connection. Every decision it makes is logged in detail — on the watch, for you, so the thresholds can be tuned against your real sessions. None of it goes anywhere.

Where it is

The control law, the Spotify client and the whole watch interface are built and tested. What is left is the part that cannot be hurried.

Nothing has run on a wrist yet. There is nothing to install and no date — when there is, it will be here.
