The promise

Three things it is for.

  • You never touch it mid-effort. No skipping to find something with the right energy, no reaching for a phone at threshold. The loop closes itself.
  • It works with the phone left at home. Everything runs on the watch: heart rate, decisions, playback. The phone is needed once, to connect Spotify, and never again.
  • The taste stays yours. Five playlists, five prompts you write. The system supplies the timing; you supply what Tempo actually means.

How it decides

Effort is the input. Energy is the output.

Heart rate is already measured continuously and accurately on the wrist, and it is a direct proxy for effort. Effort maps onto a small number of music energy tiers. bio-rhythm closes the loop between the two, so nothing needs adjusting mid-run.

The five pools are Spotify playlists you create with your own prompts. What Tempo means is whatever your Tempo prompt returned from your library — the system supplies the timing, you supply the taste.

The guarantee

It cannot cut off a song.

Not will not — cannot. The automatic controller is handed an interface that has exactly two things on it: read the playback state, and add a track to the queue. Pause, skip, previous and seek are not merely unused; they are unnameable from where the decision is made. A skip from control logic is a compile error rather than a bug report.

The decision waits until the last twenty seconds so it reflects the effort you are actually making, then retries at T−14s and T−9s if the network fails. Past T−6s it gives up and logs a miss, because a commit that lands after the boundary does nothing at all.

What it does not do

There is no server. There is no account.

  • No backend. The watch talks to Spotify directly. Nothing is relayed through anyone else's machine, because there is no machine to relay it through.
  • No account. You connect your own Spotify. There is nothing to sign up for.
  • No telemetry leaving the device. Every decision is logged in detail — on the watch, for you, so the thresholds can be tuned against real sessions. None of it is uploaded.
  • No workout tracking. It holds the device's one workout session, so it saves the workout to Health with your heart rate and ring credit. It is not a training log and does not try to be.

Where it is

Most of the way there.

The control law, the Spotify client and the whole watch interface are built and tested. What is left is the part that cannot be hurried: the loop has to run through real workouts before it is trusted to choose anything.

Every line of this has been verified by continuous integration and by nothing else. No session has yet taken place on a wrist. There is nothing to install and no date — when there is, it will be said here.

The next step is deliberately slow, and that is the point. Several real workouts get logged with the loop watching but not acting; the timing constants get tuned against those logs before anything is allowed to touch playback. Ten consecutive sessions with zero interruptions is the bar for turning it on — because a system that cuts off one song in twenty is not a system anybody wants running unattended.
